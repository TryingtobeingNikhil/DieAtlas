Where does a byte live? Tag, index, offset

Lesson · 6 steps · ~5 min
Address the core wants: 0x7FFE3A48
▬ L1 data cache

The cache doesn't search everywhere. The middle bits of the address, called the index, pick exactly one row, called a set. Here that's set 41.

Only the 8 slots in that row get checked. That's why the lookup takes about 4–5 cycles, not thousands.

Go deeper: how many sets?
32 KB
8
64 B
✕ Not a power of two, so the index can't be a clean group of bits. Real designs avoid this. Intel's Golden Cove uses 48 KB with 12 ways: 48 KB ÷ (12 × 64 B) = 64 sets.
Default: AMD Zen 4 L1D (32 KB, 8-way, 64 B lines).
